## 1. Protagonist

Patch — blacksmith, precision smith, gender player's choice, home village player's choice. Full profile in MOSTLY_CANON.md §3.

## 2. Backstory the player never sees (AUTHOR TRUTH)

The Forge has run for ~1,500 years on Spooky Fluid in hollow spheres, tended by the Order of the Most Sacred Mystery at the Abbey of the Hopefully Infinite Thrum. Its "routine service interval" is 180 days; nobody alive knows that's what the monks' rituals are. The Fluid ran low; the barrier began to thin (Act One flickers). The Abbey needed new spheres and new Fluid. The request for spheres went down a chain of ordinary hands — Brack/the Abbey → Sedge → Hobb → Wren → Brindle — until Brindle handed Patch an order form. Brack, meanwhile, prepared the Fluid from the Er'side (a User's Guide the order reads as scripture), through a microscope-like device with one surviving eyepiece.

## 3. The Opening — LOCKED, Adam's prose (July 7) `[D13]`

This is the canonical opening. Docs/02's "five days" and cart-building beats are superseded/rejected. Reproduced verbatim because it is the voice reference for the whole game:

> *Patch's eyes were narrowed and his tongue was sticking out a little bit in concentration while he finished the final polishing on the very last sphere. Finally done after six days of focus on the task, he carefully placed the sphere into a crate with the rest of the order. After sealing the crate closed, he walked to his kitchen table to get the order form so he could give instructions to the courier.*
>
> *He approached the table and noticed the water cup on its side. He noticed the cat sitting there unperturbed. "hmf." Damn cat simply could not leave a cup unturned. THEN he noticed the order form. Halfway into the puddle of water. The delivery location was gone. "HMF." Patch knew better than to leave important paperwork in range of the damn cat, but he'd been so stressed by the timeline of the spheres contract that he felt compelled to start work as SOON as Brindle left the smithy, and thinking back, he was pretty sure he'd really only stopped to eat and sleep since then.*
>
> *He glances at the required delivery date at the top of the order form. 8 more days, and he knew that the spheres were going to somewhere in The Turning, so he should have plenty of time to get to Near Enough to ask Brindle where exactly they needed to go, and be back to give the instructions to the courier, he'd just have to pay them for an express delivery, which annoyed Patch but after some quick math on a (dry) notebook, he confirmed that he'd still make a profit.*
>
> *He packs a bag with some supplies for the half-day trek to Near Enough, and glances at his home-made mechanical calendar, and notices that none of the gears are turning. It's stopped running, and he has no idea when that happened. "HNNNNGGGGG." Extremely irritated but also immediately thinking of what could have gone wrong and how to fix it, he runs to the front door, sticks his head out, and shouts asking for the date. Several of his neighbors and a few guards all call back. Something about what they said was alarming but he couldn't put his finger on what. Stuff like that usually bugged him so he stopped for a second to figure out what the problem was.*
>
> *Patch goes pale. He hadn't been working for six days, he'd been working for NINE. Something didn't seem quite right about that. Yes, he'd been extremely focused and single-minded, but Patch wasn't the kind of person to lose track of details, especially something as obvious as "how many days have i been working on these spheres?"*
>
> *Well, he had no time right now to figure out what was happening, he only knew that he was already late to get the order delivered on time. He had NEVER in his entire career been late to deliver an order, and he wasn't going to start today. He'd have to move fast. An express courier could make the journey, but only if they pushed hard — no stopping except to water the horse, no comfortable inns, sleeping in the wagon if at all. Patch had done it before. He could do it again. He added more supplies to his pack, loaded the crates, hitched the horse, and set out. Hopefully Brindle would remember the delivery location.*

## 5. Act One — The Delivery Route (the chain, upward)

Order of encounter: Brindle → Wren → Hobb → Sedge → the Abbey. Each link is LOCKED at the July 7 level; encounter *prose* does not exist; Aug 8 character sheets are PROVISIONAL (see §6).

**Near Enough — Brindle** `[E2]`. Half a day's walk. Brindle handed Patch the order and remembers roughly where it came from; he is rattled by a mill running against the river current and sends Patch to look. **The backward mill** `[G14]` LOCKED: "First world weirdness errand. Mechanical, observable, partially fixable. The fix works. The reason it was running backwards is never fully explained." Brindle introduces shops.

**The trading post — Wren** `[E3]`. Halfway between Near Enough and the NW border of The Hold. Looking for a missing river. Gives Patch the first map (bird in the corner). Source of all maps; map notes evolve as the world breaks.

**The perfectly normal village — Hobb** `[E4]`. Near The Turning border. Sky blue, no glitches. Hobb's inn; the rumor mechanic. Outskirts: a miller with a stopped mill and a ferryman with no river — both helpable. Hobb's rumors are the most accurate in the world because his village is unaffected; "Patch will never know or appreciate this."

**Still Point — Sedge** `[E5]`. In The Turning; nothing rotates; residents unsettled and won't leave. Sedge on a six-month rotation the order has kept for centuries for a forgotten reason; monks find it "profoundly stressful"; nothing is obviously wrong. The watching-for-what conversation "will bother Patch for the rest of the game." Sedge points Patch to the Abbey.

**Anchor-and-fill guarantees** for this route: a midpoint rest, a border crossing, a first contact with outer weirdness, a destination; every chain link always exists and is reachable. `[G1]`

Throughout: the weirdness, deniable → undeniable. On first play, atmosphere; on replay, obviously the unraveling.
