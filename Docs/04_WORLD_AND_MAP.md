# MOSTLY — Game Design Document

## Document 4: World & Map Design

-----

## World Structure Overview

The world is circular. The Hold occupies the center. Six outer regions radiate outward as irregular wedge shapes. Stub regions exist beyond the named regions — implied on the map but containing no content at launch. The map is the primary visual signal of what's finished and what's still an invitation — the stub regions are the endgame module roadmap.

-----

## The Hold

### Names

- **Official name:** The Principality of the Mostly Peaceful Interior
- **Common name:** The Mostly
- **Folk name:** The Hold *(origin unknown, predates written record)*

The folk name “The Hold” is older than anyone remembers. The origin is forgotten. Nobody connects it to The Forge’s function of holding back the paranormal fringe — except possibly a very old historian or a very drunk abbot who has always found the etymology curious.

### Character

Boring. Safe. Agrarian. Communal by necessity rather than warmth. The people generally seem content but there is a subtle undercurrent of restlessness — something not quite right that nobody can put a finger on.

**NPC archetypes:**

- The contented majority: haven’t noticed, are fine
- The willfully ignorant: have noticed, performing fineness, react badly to questions
- The street prophets: know something is wrong, garbled signal, mix of real knowledge and fear — not wrong, just not quite right
- The rare sane few: have been quietly waiting for someone to actually ask; not marked, not glowing, found through exploration and exhausted dialogue

### Geography

**True center:** Untouched wilderness. Something is there. Details deferred. The administrative capital was placed slightly northeast of center because building at the exact center “seemed suspicious.” This decision is in the founding documents. Nobody questions it anymore.

**The circle:** Within the true center wilderness, ancient engineers drew a circle. Most people cannot see it. Children refuse to play there. Dogs walk around it. The grass is completely normal. Probably.

**Biome bleed:** Every outer region contributes at least one biome that bleeds across the border into The Hold. The Hold is not sealed — it is just the part where people decided to live anyway.

**Scale:** Approximately one week’s journey across.

### The Capital: Margin

**Official name:** Margin
**Position:** Slightly northeast of true center

Every building sits just outside a circle drawn by ancient engineers. Nobody wanted to build inside it. Nobody can say why. Children refuse to play there. Dogs walk around it. The grass is completely normal. Probably.

The city is built on a grid that is perfectly regular except slightly rotated from true North/South, causing street signs to require extra reinforcement.

**Notable street:** East South East Slightly Left of Center Avenue

#### Neighborhoods

- **Upper Filing** — administrative district, senior civil servants
- **Lower Filing** — working administrative district
- **East Upper Lower** — named by someone who just described its location
- **Records** — nothing but archives; no one knows how large it actually is
- **Temporary Annex** — 700 years old; contains the Royal Palace; nobody moved it
- **Overflow** — self-explanatory

#### Government Buildings

**The Ministry of Normal Things**
Largest ministry in the realm. Responsible for reassuring people. Has never solved a crime. Has accidentally prevented seventeen apocalypses.

**The Office of Unlikely Events**
Open 24 hours. Always busy. The queue includes people who have been waiting weeks. Some aren’t sure their event qualifies. Staff has seen everything and reacts to nothing.

**The Department of Acceptable Distances**
Maintains laws regarding: cemeteries, mirrors, abandoned wells, standing stones, suspicious mushrooms, and “anything that hums.” Somewhere in their records are distance regulations that imply someone once measured the safe distance from The Forge and filed it without comment.

**The Bureau of Rotational Affairs**
Nobody knows what they do. Everyone agrees they are extremely important. Leave permanently unexplained.

## Outer Regions

### Regional Structure

Each region contains:

- One administrative border city (fixed per region, placed just inside the regional border)
- 2-3 biomes (generated per run from the region’s biome pool)
- Multiple smaller settlements (names drawn from biome-appropriate lists)
- One monastic order and settlement: **The [Badass Noun]**
- Folklore about the region (what people in The Hold believe)
- Reality (what’s actually true — sometimes mundane and hilarious, sometimes genuinely dangerous for wrong reasons)

Border administrative cities are Margin’s outposts — built so civil servants can technically say they’ve been to the outer lands. Full of administrators on two-week postings, restaurants serving Hold food, and nervous Ministry of Normal Things satellite offices.

### Biome-Appropriate City Flavor

Each border city has fixed name and identity. Its specific character (neighborhoods, street names, NPC tone) draws from a biome-appropriate list based on what generated around it that run.

## Stub Regions

Exist on the map as labeled space with no content at launch. Implied by:

- Unlabeled space visible on the world map
- NPC folklore references
- Code stubs in game files (visible to modders)
- Possible offhand mentions in dialogue

Stub regions are filled primarily by studio-authored endgame modules. Optional post-launch community modding, if it launches, is a possible second source using the same module data format — not a base-game dependency either way.

**Tone, locked August 2026: stub regions are the tonal release valve.** The main story stays disciplined deadpan — real stakes, straight-faced delivery. Stub-region and endgame-module content is explicitly permitted to be weirder and sillier, tongue-in-cheek in a way the mainline story never is. This isn't a tone break; it's earned by what stub regions *are* diegetically — unfinished, unfiled, half-real edges of the map that nobody in-world has gotten around to explaining properly. The bureaucratic-nonsense register already established for The Hold (Ministry of Normal Things, Bureau of Rotational Affairs) is the right seed to grow this from, just turned up.

**The stub region is not missing content — it is an invitation.**

## Map Generation (Anchor-and-Fill)

### Fixed Elements (Every Run)

- The Hold at center
- Six outer regions in fixed positions
- Stub regions beyond named regions
- Nine starting villages within The Hold
- The Forge in The Turning
- Glorp Abbey / The Grinding near The Forge
- Margin slightly northeast of center
- True center wilderness

### Generated Elements (Per Run)

- Which biomes appear within each region
- Which biomes bleed into The Hold at each border
- Names of smaller settlements (drawn from biome-appropriate pools)
- Specific locations of touchpoints within regions
- The flavor/character of border administrative cities

### Critical Path Anchors

Each narrative path has fixed touchpoints that always generate regardless of biome configuration:

**Delivery Route anchors:** midpoint rest, border crossing, first contact with outer weirdness, destination

**Spooky Fluid Chain anchors:** each link in the commission chain always exists and is always reachable — the people who passed along the order, culminating in the person who botched the Fluid

**Path to The Forge anchors:** always navigable; The Grinding always accessible; The Forge always findable

### Anchor Placement Constraints

- Minimum distance between anchors (prevents trivially short biome fill)
- Maximum distance between anchors (prevents losing narrative coherence across biome transitions)
- Implementation detail: minimum and maximum distance parameter per touchpoint type

### Content Pipeline

Conceptually a spreadsheet:

- Rows: touchpoint types
- Columns: biome types
- Each cell: 2-3 biome-appropriate encounters

Early builds have sparse cells and repetition. Later builds have full cells and variety. Modders add rows. Stub region columns are empty and waiting.

## Biome Types

Available biomes for region generation:

|Biome         |Character                                                                                                         |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Plains        |Flat, agricultural, unremarkable                                                                                  |
|Savanna       |Dry grassland, wide horizons                                                                                      |
|Mountain      |Cold air, difficult terrain                                                                                       |
|Cold          |Harsh, isolated, everything takes longer                                                                          |
|Desert        |Sparse, disorienting, things hidden by distance                                                                   |
|Wetlands/Marsh|Slow, murky, things hidden beneath the surface                                                                    |
|Dense Forest  |Old growth, canopy blocks sky, sounds carry wrong                                                                 |
|Badlands      |Eroded, strange rock formations, implies geological drama                                                         |
|Coastal       |Edge of the known world; ships; things from further out                                                           |
|Volcanic      |Active geology; unstable; folklore blames something supernatural; reality is tectonic; Patch finds this refreshing|

Rivers and other landscape features distributed throughout.
